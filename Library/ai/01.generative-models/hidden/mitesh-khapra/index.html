Statement of Research Interests

My long-term interest lies in understanding what it would take for machine learning systems to genuinely think and reason, rather than merely fit patterns in data. I believe this question cannot be answered from the outside — it requires first understanding what is actually happening inside the models we already have. As an incoming MS by Research student in the department, this is the conviction that shapes my research direction: building an intuition-first understanding of how Transformer-based architectures generalize, and uncovering the functional roles of their internal components.

Over the past year, I have been pursuing this question through Connecting the Dots, a project where I document my own ground-up exploration of attention and Transformer internals — building intuition incrementally and probing how models process information, for example how individual attention heads behave and how representations evolve across layers. This is not coursework; it is a personal attempt to get closer to the bigger question of how understanding, or something like it, emerges inside these systems.

My undergraduate education at NITK Surathkal in Electrical and Electronics Engineering provided a robust mathematical and computational foundation through coursework in machine learning, neural networks, statistics, and signal processing. Alongside this, I engaged in rigorous self-study—working through CS7015, Stanford's CS229, CS224N, and CS236, as well as foundational texts like Deep Learning (Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville) and Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction (Sutton & Barto). Applying these concepts practically, such as building scientific machine learning simulations in PyTorch for inverse parameter estimation of Turing patterns, solidified my passion for analyzing a model's underlying mechanics.

I was excited to find that some of the questions I've been probing in Connecting the Dots closely align with your lab's own work on the interpretability of attention mechanisms in Transformer models. Studies examining the faithfulness of attention explanations, analyses of local versus long-range information in token representations, and work probing the relationship between attention head importance and prunability reflect the exact kind of research I aim to undertake. I view a model's internal structure as a fundamental and rich object of study in its own right.

Now that I have joined the program, I am eager to collaboratively shape a specific research problem in this space. I bring a genuine commitment to this subfield, a solid technical foundation, and the dedication required to understand complex systems deeply to drive meaningful innovations.

I believe your lab would provide the ideal environment for my thesis work. I would appreciate the opportunity to discuss how my background and interests align with your ongoing projects, and to explore the possibility of working under your guidance.

If you would like to see how I approach problems and think about deep learning concepts, please check out my interactive probing experiments or explore my pedagogical project, Connecting the Dots.
