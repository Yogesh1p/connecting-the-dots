Statement of Research Interests

My long-term interest lies in building intelligent agents that can learn, reason, and make optimal decisions in complex, dynamic, or structured environments. I believe this requires combining the strengths of deep representation learning with reinforcement learning and graph structures. As an incoming MS by Research student in the department, this is the conviction that shapes my research direction: developing agent-based models that learn robust, structured representations of their environments to generalize better and make decisions more efficiently.

Over the past year, I have been exploring neural network representations through Connecting the Dots, a project where I document my own ground-up exploration of deep learning models. While my work so far has focused on Transformer internals, I am eager to apply these representation learning concepts to RL agents and structured domains like graphs, developing models that can learn to abstract and generalize effectively.

My undergraduate education at NITK Surathkal in Electrical and Electronics Engineering provided a robust mathematical and computational foundation through coursework in machine learning, neural networks, statistics, and signal processing. Alongside this, I engaged in rigorous self-study—working through Sutton & Barto's Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, Stanford's CS229, CS224N, CS236, as well as foundational texts like Deep Learning (Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville). Applying these concepts practically, such as building scientific machine learning simulations in PyTorch for inverse parameter estimation of Turing patterns, solidified my passion for analyzing a model's underlying mechanics.

I am particularly excited by your lab's research at the Wadhwani School of Data Science and AI on Reinforcement Learning (including multi-agent RL, safe RL, and hierarchical RL) and Graph Neural Networks. Studies exploring representation learning on graphs, explainability in RL, and agent-environment interactions reflect the exact kind of research I aim to undertake. I view representation learning as a crucial bridge to building scalable, explainable, and sample-efficient RL agents.

Now that I have joined the program, I am eager to collaboratively shape a specific research problem in this space. I bring a genuine commitment to this subfield, a solid technical foundation, and the dedication required to understand complex systems deeply to drive meaningful innovations.

I believe your lab would provide the ideal environment for my thesis work. I would appreciate the opportunity to discuss how my background and interests align with your ongoing projects, and to explore the possibility of working under your guidance.

If you would like to see how I approach problems and think about deep learning concepts, please check out my interactive probing experiments or explore my pedagogical project, Connecting the Dots.
